# Sourdough Baking

Sourdough bread is leavened by a culture of wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria instead of
commercial yeast. The culture is called a starter, and keeping it healthy is most of the work.

## Feeding the starter

A starter is a mix of flour and water that ferments at room temperature. Feed it by discarding
most of it and adding fresh flour and water in equal weights, for example 50 g starter, 50 g
flour and 50 g water. A starter that lives on the counter needs feeding once or twice a day. If
you bake only once a week, keep the jar in the fridge and feed it once a week; take it out the
evening before baking and give it two feedings so it becomes active again. A ripe starter has
doubled in volume, smells pleasantly sour and is full of bubbles.

## Hydration and mixing

Hydration is the weight of water divided by the weight of flour. A dough with 1000 g flour and
700 g water has 70 % hydration. Higher hydration gives an open, irregular crumb but is sticky and
harder to shape; beginners should start around 65 to 70 %. Mix flour and water first and let them
rest for thirty minutes – this rest is called autolyse – before adding starter and salt.

## Bulk fermentation and proofing

During bulk fermentation the dough rises in one piece. Instead of kneading, perform a set of
stretch and folds every half hour for the first two hours. Bulk fermentation is finished when the
dough has grown by roughly half and shows bubbles on the sides of the container; at 24 °C this
takes four to six hours. After shaping, the loaf proofs in a basket, often overnight in the fridge.
A cold proof improves flavour and makes scoring easier.

## Baking in a Dutch oven

Preheat the oven with a cast-iron Dutch oven inside to 250 °C. Turn the cold loaf onto parchment,
score it with a razor blade and bake it covered for twenty minutes so the trapped steam lets the
crust expand. Remove the lid and bake another twenty to twenty-five minutes until the crust is
deep brown. Let the bread cool for at least an hour before cutting it, otherwise the crumb turns
gummy.
