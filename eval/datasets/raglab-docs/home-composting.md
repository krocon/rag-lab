# Home Composting

Composting turns kitchen and garden waste into a dark, crumbly soil improver. Microorganisms do
the work; your job is to give them the right food, air and moisture.

## Greens and browns

Compost needs a balance of nitrogen-rich "greens" and carbon-rich "browns". Greens are vegetable
scraps, coffee grounds, fresh grass clippings and plant trimmings. Browns are dry leaves, straw,
shredded cardboard and wood chips. Aim for roughly two to three parts browns to one part greens by
volume. Do not add meat, dairy, oily food or pet waste to a home heap.

## Moisture and turning

The heap should feel like a wrung-out sponge. Add water to a dry heap and more browns to a soggy
one. Turning the material with a fork every week or two brings in oxygen and speeds things up; a hot
heap can reach 60 °C in its centre, which kills most weed seeds.

## Troubleshooting smells

A rotten-egg or ammonia smell means the heap has too many greens or too little air. Mix in dry
browns and turn it. Fruit flies are kept away by burying fresh scraps under a layer of browns. A
heap that stays cold is usually too small, too dry or short of greens.

## Using finished compost

Compost is ready after three to twelve months, when it smells earthy and the original materials
are no longer recognisable. Sieve out large pieces and return them to the next heap. Spread a few
centimetres on beds in spring or mix it into potting soil.
