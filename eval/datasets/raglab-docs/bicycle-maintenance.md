# Bicycle Maintenance

A few minutes of regular care keep a bicycle safe and make it last for many years.

## Tyre pressure

Check tyre pressure every week, because tubes lose air slowly even without a puncture. The allowed
range is printed on the tyre sidewall. Road tyres typically run at 5.5 to 7 bar, mountain bike
tyres at 1.5 to 2.2 bar. Too little pressure causes pinch flats and sluggish handling; too much
reduces grip on rough surfaces.

## Cleaning and lubricating the chain

A dirty chain wears out the cassette and chainrings. Wipe the chain with a rag after wet rides,
degrease it every few hundred kilometres, and apply one drop of chain lubricant to each roller.
Wipe off the excess afterwards: oil on the outside of the chain only attracts grit. Measure chain
wear with a chain checker and replace the chain at 0.5 % elongation.

## Brakes

Rim brake pads have wear grooves; replace them when the grooves are gone. Disc brake pads should be
replaced when the friction material is thinner than about 1 mm. Squealing disc brakes are often
contaminated with oil – clean the rotor with isopropyl alcohol. A brake lever that pulls to the
handlebar needs cable adjustment or, for hydraulic brakes, bleeding.

## Fixing a puncture

Remove the wheel, lever one side of the tyre off the rim and pull out the tube. Find the cause by
running your fingers carefully along the inside of the tyre, then patch the tube or fit a new one.
Inflate it slightly before refitting so it does not get pinched between tyre and rim.
