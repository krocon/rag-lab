Houseplant Care

Most houseplants die from too much attention rather than too little. Understanding light and water
prevents nearly all common problems.

Light is the most important factor. A plant that grows long, pale stems leaning towards the window
is not getting enough light. Succulents and cacti want a south-facing window, while ferns and
calatheas prefer bright but indirect light. Rotate pots a quarter turn every few weeks so they grow
evenly.

Water thoroughly and then let the top two or three centimetres of soil dry out before watering
again. Yellow, soft lower leaves and a sour smell from the pot are typical signs of overwatering
and root rot. Always use pots with a drainage hole and empty the saucer after watering. Plants need
much less water in winter, when growth slows down.

Repot a plant when roots grow out of the drainage hole or circle densely around the root ball,
usually every one to two years in spring. Choose a pot only two to four centimetres wider than the
old one; a much larger pot holds too much wet soil.

Feed with a diluted liquid fertiliser during the growing season, roughly once a month from spring
to late summer, and not at all in winter. Brown, crispy leaf tips often come from dry indoor air or
from salt build-up caused by fertiliser; flushing the soil with plenty of water helps.
