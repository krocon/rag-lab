# Marathon Training

Finishing a marathon depends far more on months of consistent, mostly easy running than on any
single hard workout.

## Building a base

Before starting a sixteen- to twenty-week plan, you should be able to run about 30 km per week
comfortably. Increase weekly mileage gradually – a common guideline is no more than ten percent per
week – and schedule an easier recovery week every third or fourth week. Most runs should be at a
conversational pace.

## The long run

The weekly long run is the backbone of the plan. Extend it step by step to 30 or 32 km about four
weeks before the race. Run it slowly, one to two minutes per kilometre slower than goal race pace,
and use it to rehearse fuelling and the shoes and clothes you will wear on race day.

## Fuelling and hydration

During runs longer than ninety minutes the body needs carbohydrates: about 30 to 60 g per hour, for
example from energy gels taken every 30 to 40 minutes with water. Drink to thirst rather than on a
fixed schedule; drinking far too much can dilute blood sodium. Practise your race-day nutrition in
training so the stomach gets used to it.

## Tapering

In the final two to three weeks reduce weekly volume by roughly 20 to 50 percent while keeping a few
short faster sections. The taper lets muscles repair and glycogen stores fill up. Feeling restless
during the taper is normal; resist adding extra workouts.
