# Coffee Brewing

Good coffee at home depends on fresh beans, the right grind size and a consistent ratio of coffee
to water.

## Grind size

The finer the grind, the faster water extracts flavour. Espresso needs a fine grind similar to
powdered sugar, pour-over a medium grind like sand, and the French press a coarse grind. Coffee that
tastes sour and thin is under-extracted: grind finer. Coffee that tastes bitter and harsh is
over-extracted: grind coarser. A burr grinder produces far more even particles than a blade grinder.

## Brew ratio and water

A good starting ratio for filter coffee is 60 g of coffee per litre of water, about 1 to 16. Weigh
both instead of using scoops. Water just off the boil, around 92 to 96 °C, works for most methods.
Because coffee is mostly water, use filtered water if your tap water tastes of chlorine or is very
hard.

## Pour-over method

Rinse the paper filter with hot water to remove the papery taste and warm the brewer. Add the
ground coffee, pour about twice its weight of water and wait thirty seconds while the coffee blooms
and releases carbon dioxide. Then pour the rest slowly in circles. The total brew time should be
about three to four minutes.

## Espresso basics

Espresso forces hot water through a compact puck of finely ground coffee at about 9 bar. A classic
double shot uses 18 g of coffee and yields about 36 g of liquid in 25 to 30 seconds. If the shot runs
much faster, grind finer; if it barely drips, grind coarser.
