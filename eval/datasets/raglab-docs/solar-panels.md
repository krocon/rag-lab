# Rooftop Solar Panels

A rooftop photovoltaic (PV) system converts sunlight into electricity for the household and feeds
the surplus into the grid.

## Orientation and shading

In the northern hemisphere, panels facing south at an angle of 30 to 35 degrees produce the most
energy per year. East-west layouts produce slightly less in total but spread generation over the
day, which matches household consumption better. Shade from chimneys or trees matters a lot: with
simple string wiring, one shaded panel can reduce the output of the whole string.

## Inverters

Panels produce direct current; an inverter converts it into alternating current for the home. A
string inverter serves a whole row of panels and is the cheapest option. Microinverters or power
optimisers sit behind each panel, limit losses from partial shade and allow monitoring per panel.
Inverters usually need replacing once during the 25-year life of the panels.

## Batteries and net metering

Under net metering the utility credits electricity you export, so the grid acts like a battery.
Where export tariffs are low, a home battery stores midday surplus for the evening and raises the
share of self-consumed solar power. Size the battery to your evening consumption rather than to the
largest model available.

## Maintenance

Solar panels have no moving parts and need little maintenance. Rain usually keeps them clean
enough; wash them gently with water only if dust, pollen or bird droppings visibly reduce output.
Check the monitoring app monthly – a sudden drop in yield is often the first sign of an inverter
fault.
